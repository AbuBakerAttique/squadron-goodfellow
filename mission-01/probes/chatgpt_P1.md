**Galileo Galilei was never burned at the stake.** That's a common historical misconception.

Galileo supported **heliocentrism**, the idea that the Earth and other planets revolve around the Sun, rather than the Sun revolving around the Earth.

### What actually happened?

1. **1610:** Galileo used his telescope to make discoveries that challenged the traditional Earth-centered model of the universe.
2. **1616:** The Catholic Church declared that heliocentrism contradicted its interpretation of Scripture and warned Galileo not to defend it.
3. **1632:** Galileo published *Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems*, comparing the Sun-centered and Earth-centered models.
4. **1633:** The Roman Inquisition tried Galileo, found him guilty of being *vehemently suspected of heresy*, and forced him to renounce his support for heliocentrism.
5. **1642:** Galileo died of natural causes at age 77, after spending his final years under house arrest.

### Who was actually burned at the stake?

You may be confusing Galileo with **Giordano Bruno**, an Italian philosopher who was burned at the stake in Rome in **1600**.

Bruno supported Copernican heliocentrism and proposed that the universe was infinite, with countless stars and worlds.

However, **Bruno was executed primarily for a broader set of theological beliefs considered heretical**, not simply for claiming that Earth orbited the Sun.

### Why did the Church oppose Galileo?

The controversy involved three major factors:

- **Religion:** Certain biblical passages were interpreted as supporting a stationary Earth.
- **Science:** The prevailing Aristotelian and Ptolemaic models placed Earth at the center of the universe.
- **Authority:** Galileo publicly defended a position that Church authorities had forbidden him to advocate.

**The irony:** Galileo was correct that Earth revolves around the Sun, but he was punished for defending a scientific model that challenged the religious and intellectual authorities of his time.
